02 / Founder story

It started with one fixer.

Africa has the stories. Africa has the talent. But producing in Africa can still be unnecessarily difficult.

The story began in 2013, when founder Obaji Akpet, then working as a journalist in Cross River State and freelancing with The Nation, began working as a fixer for international media.

That experience grew into years of work across journalism, documentary production and commercial production, supporting organisations including BBC, Reuters, Al Jazeera, Insider, TotalEnergies, HP, Nike and FIFA, among others.

Over time, the need became clear. Africa Fixers was created to build a more connected, professional and dependable way to produce across the continent.

What a production fixer actually does

A fixer is the person who understands the location and can make things happen on the ground. Here is what that covers, and when a production needs more than one person.

By Africa FixersDraft2 min read

Producing in a new country can mean navigating permits, locations, people, transport, equipment, access, language, culture and logistics, often while working against tight deadlines. A fixer is the person who brings those moving parts together.

Africa Fixers began with exactly that work. In 2013, our founder Obaji Akpet, then a journalist in Cross River State freelancing with The Nation, began working as a fixer for international media. Everything we do today grew from it.

What fixing covers

Fixing is local coordination, research and problem-solving from people who understand the terrain. Depending on the production, it can include:

  • Research and access: identifying contributors, arranging interviews and reaching the people, organisations and places your story requires.
  • Locations: finding places that fit the creative and practical requirements, then coordinating access.
  • Permits: navigating local permissions, filming requirements and government or institutional processes.
  • Logistics: vehicles, accommodation and the practical requirements that keep a team moving.
  • Cultural context: knowing how to approach people and places, and who to call when plans change.
  • Day-to-day production assistance once the shoot is underway.

Why local knowledge matters

Producing in an unfamiliar environment requires more than finding someone on the ground. It requires people who understand the local environment, know who to call, understand production and can solve problems when plans change.

Filming requirements differ from country to country and location to location. The fixer is often the difference between a plan that works on paper and one that works on the day.

When you need more than a fixer

Some productions need one experienced fixer. Others need a complete local production unit: producers, production managers, cinematographers, camera crew, sound professionals, production assistants, drivers, researchers, translators and interpreters, location specialists and technical specialists.

We can provide individual crew members or build a complete team around your brief, from a single fixer to full local production and co-production.

Know the terrain. Know the people. Make it happen.

What to send before your shoot (and what can wait)

You do not need everything figured out before you get in touch. These are the details that let us plan properly, and the ones we can help you work out.

By Africa FixersDraft2 min read

You do not need to have everything figured out before contacting us. Give us what you know, and we will help identify what is needed to make the production happen.

That said, the more of the picture we have early, the sooner we can plan properly. These are the details that make the biggest difference.

The essentials

  • Where: the country or countries, and specific locations if you have them.
  • When: your production dates, even if they are provisional.
  • What: the production type: documentary, news, commercial, branded content, corporate or something else.
  • Who is travelling: the number of crew coming in, so we can plan logistics around them.

What will help us plan

  • Services required: fixing, locations, permits, crew, equipment, transport, research, filming or full production.
  • The brief: the story, the people or places you need to reach, and anything that makes the shoot unusual.
  • Budget, if available: it helps us recommend the right scale of support from the start.

What can wait

Exact locations, contributors, permit routes and kit lists are often what we help you work out. Identifying the people, locations, equipment, permits and logistics required is the second step of how we work, not something you need to solve first.

Tell us where you are filming, when you are filming and what you need.

How to send it

The project inquiry form on our contact page asks for each of these details. If you prefer, send the same information by email or WhatsApp. Either way, we will review your brief and come back with a clear proposal.
